# 2. Mechanical drive train

The mechanical drive train of a bicycle has three main components:

* The front crank set, with one or more chain rings.
* The rear cassette, with one or more sprockets.
* The chain used to connect the selected chain ring and sprocket.
  
The operation of the drive train is ruled by the laws of classical physics, mainly:

* Circular Motion.
* Conservation of Energy.
  
The pedaling motion of the rider generates a torque on the chain ring:

$$\tau_{cr} = F_{pedal} \times r_{cr}$$

where $$F_{pedal}$$ is the force (in Newtons) the leg exerts on the pedal, and $$r_{cr}$$ is the radius (in meters) of the chain ring.

The actual power (in Watts) generated by the rider is:

P = $$\tau_{cr} \times \omega_{cr}$$

where $$\omega_{cr}$$ is the angular velocity (in radians per second) of the chain ring.

The angular velocity of the chain ring can be computed from the pedaling cadence C (in RPM) as follows:

$$\omega = C \times \frac{2\pi \text{ rad}}{60 \text{ s}}$$
